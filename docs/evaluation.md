### Wall-Clock Timing (Adult, d=104)

| Operation | n=1K | n=5K | n=10K | n=30K |

| `fit()` | 0.01s | 0.06s | 0.04s | 0.09s |

| `sample_hitandrun(50)` | 0.3s | 1.3s | 2.1s | 5.2s |

Fitting and ellipsoid sampling scale linearly with n. Hit-and-Run is the
bottleneck because each step requires an $O(nd)$ matrix-vector product for
the line search. Memory is dominated by the data matrix: 25.9 MB for the
full Adult dataset.

## Ellipsoid Tightness Analysis

The ellipsoidal approximation $L(\hat\theta + \Delta) \approx L(\hat\theta) +
\frac{1}{2}\Delta^T H \Delta$ underpins all certificate-based computations
(hacking intervals, probability bands, ambiguity bounds). We compare the
**certificate width** (analytic maximum over the ellipsoid) to the **empirical
width** (range observed across Hit-and-Run samples) to assess how tight the
approximation is.

### Interpretation

The certificate-to-empirical ratio depends strongly on dimensionality:

| d | Ratio range | Assessment |
|--:|:-----------:|:-----------|
| 10 | 1.3-1.4x | Tight. Certificates are nearly exact. |
| 30 | 4-9x | Moderate. Certificates are valid upper bounds with useful tightness. |
| 104 | 20-38x | Conservative. Use HR for tighter empirical estimates. |

**100% fidelity** across all experiments confirms that every ellipsoid sample
satisfies the true membership oracle. The certificates are valid upper bounds
in all cases. The gap at high d arises from two sources:

1. **Worst-case direction**: the certificate reports the maximum over all
   directions, while typical instances project onto lower-variance subspaces.
2. **HR underexploration**: 200 Hit-and-Run samples in 104 dimensions cover
   only a fraction of the set. The empirical width is an underestimate.

**Practical guidance**: In $d < 30$, use certificates for fast, useful bounds.
In $d > 50$, use certificates as conservative upper bounds and supplement
with Hit-and-Run for tighter empirical estimates (allocating sufficient samples).
