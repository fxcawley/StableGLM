## Summary

For each dataset, C is selected by 5-fold cross-validation. Both certificate-based
(ellipsoidal, fast) and empirical (Hit-and-Run, exact) ambiguity are reported.

| Dataset | d | C (CV) | Acc | Cert. Amb. | Emp. Amb. | Cert/Emp | Min ESS |
|:--------|--:|-------:|----:|-----------:|----------:|---------:|--------:|
| BC PCA-10 | 10 | 10.0 | 94.4% | **36.0%** | **27.0%** | 1.3x | 203 |
| BC Full | 30 | 0.5 | 95.3% | **18.4%** | **10.0%** | 1.8x | 60 |
| German Credit | 61 | 1.0 | 70.2% | **85.4%** | **11.4%** | 7.5x | 23 |
| Adult Census | 104 | 0.5 | 77.1% | **79.0%** | **9.2%** | 8.6x | 3 |

**How to read this table.** The certificate ambiguity is a fast upper bound computed
from the ellipsoidal approximation. The empirical ambiguity is the ground truth from
Hit-and-Run sampling. The Cert/Emp ratio shows how conservative the certificates are.

At **d=10**, the certificate says 36% and HR confirms 27% -- a 1.3x ratio. The two
agree well, and the certificate is a fast, useful bound.

At **d=104**, the certificate says 79% but HR finds only 9.2% -- an 8.6x ratio.
The certificate is valid but conservative. Critically, the ESS of 3 means the HR
chain has not converged at 500 samples in 104 dimensions. Both the certificate
(overestimate) and the HR result (underestimate due to poor mixing) should be
interpreted with caution at this dimensionality.

**The lead result is Breast Cancer PCA-10**: 94.4% accuracy with a CV-selected C=10.0,
27% of patients have ambiguous diagnoses confirmed by 1000 well-mixed HR samples
(ESS=203), and the certificate overestimates by only 1.3x.

The certificate-to-empirical ambiguity ratio across $\epsilon$ values shows when
certificates are trustworthy.

### German Credit (d=61) -- certificates are very conservative

| $\epsilon$ | Cert. Amb. | Emp. Amb. | Ratio |
|:----------:|:----------:|:---------:|:-----:|
| 0.005 | 14.0% | 1.0% | 14.0x |
| 0.010 | 30.0% | 1.5% | 20.0x |

At d=61, the certificate overestimates ambiguity by 3-20x. The ratio improves at
larger $\epsilon$ because the ellipsoidal approximation is relatively tighter for
larger sets (the cubic error term grows slower than the quadratic).

because the ratio is a geometric property of the parameter-space ellipsoid projected
onto data directions, independent of train/test splitting.

## Practical Guidance

**d < 20:** Use certificates. They track the true ambiguity within 1.3-1.6x
and compute in milliseconds. HR is useful for confirmation but not required.

**20 < d < 60:** Certificates give valid upper bounds but overestimate by 2-8x.
Use certificates for fast screening, HR for precise estimates. Allocate
1000+ HR samples for adequate ESS.

**d > 60:** Certificates are highly conservative (8-20x). HR is necessary for
credible empirical estimates but requires very long chains (ESS < 10 at 500
samples in d=104). Consider using only the certificate pathway as a conservative
screening tool, or restrict analysis to a lower-dimensional subspace.
